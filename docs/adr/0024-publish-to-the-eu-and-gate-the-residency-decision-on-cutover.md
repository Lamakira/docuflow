# Publish to the EU and gate the residency decision on cutover

The parallel environment publishes to the **Europe (EU)** geography, and that choice is made on the asymmetry of being wrong rather than on a claim about the market — because **the market is not decided**. ADR-0016 launches DocuFlow in Frankfurt and ADR-0021 keeps that line on Replit, but neither argues for the EU: [#11](https://github.com/Lamakira/docuflow/issues/11) already asks its question "for DocuFlow's initial EU single-region deployment", so EU entered as a premise and was never examined. It is also not the only premise in the repository. ADR-0007 chooses Clerk "for the US-market-first launch", explicitly "once EU-first identity residency was no longer the launch assumption", and reserves reassessment for "an explicit EU-residency or enterprise-procurement requirement". Both cannot be the launch story. The strongest corroboration for the EU reading is ADR-0015, which is GDPR-shaped throughout — processor for workspace content, controller for account and billing, a 72-hour breach runbook, a Subprocessor Registry gated on signed DPAs and recorded regions, an Erasure Journal replayed after every restore — but that is evidence of consistency, not of a decision anyone made. This decision does not resolve the conflict. It records it, picks the placement that survives either answer, and sets the point at which the answer becomes mandatory.

**The asymmetry is the whole argument.** EU-hosted data serving North American users costs latency, which ADR-0016 already prices and mitigates with Cloudflare in front, and no law requires US or Canadian residency for ordinary B2B SaaS — a US enterprise buyer demanding it is the procurement reassessment ADR-0007 already reserves. EU personal data resting in a US region costs a restricted transfer under GDPR: permitted, but only through the EU–US Data Privacy Framework or Standard Contractual Clauses with a transfer impact assessment, per subprocessor, disclosed to customers through ADR-0015's registry — and it would block ADR-0018's rehearsal outright, since ADR-0021 and [#53](https://github.com/Lamakira/docuflow/issues/53) forbid loading a restored production snapshot into any environment outside the published geography. One direction is a latency problem with a CDN in front of it; the other is a legal programme with a standing obligation. Under genuine uncertainty the reversible direction wins, and this is not a close call.

**The window is open now and closes at cutover, which is why the gate sits there.** Geography is fixed at first publish and cannot be migrated, so a correction is always a new Replit project rather than a setting. What changes is what that project costs. Today the environment holds no production data — a repository, some Secrets, a bucket, and synthetic rehearsal data — so rebuilding it in another geography is an afternoon. After ADR-0018's cutover the same correction is a production data migration inside an announced freeze window, on the environment that by then *is* production, because ADR-0018 makes this environment the successor rather than a scratch copy. **The market and residency question is therefore a blocking input to cutover and not to this publish**: whoever owns the go-to-market answers where DocuFlow's data subjects live before traffic switches, and if the answer is North America the correction is made while the environment is still synthetic. An unanswered question at the cutover gate stops the cutover.

Three facts are recorded because each one looks like it should change the decision and does not. **The parent holding company is Canadian, and corporate domicile is not data residency** — what binds is where the data subjects are and where processing happens. Replit offers no Canadian geography at all, so "North America" is US hosting, which means choosing NA for proximity to the parent selects the one placement that is hardest to justify to either regulator; and Canada holds an EU adequacy decision for private-sector organizations under PIPEDA, so an EU deployment serves a Canadian parent's oversight without transfer paperwork while the reverse does not. If the parent sits in Quebec, Law 25 adds its own obligations for transfers outside the province, including a privacy impact assessment; that is counsel's question and is named here rather than answered. **Replit is a US company in every branch**, so vendor jurisdiction does not distinguish EU from NA — it distinguishes Replit from a European provider, which ADR-0021 already decided with its exit argument. And **the specific EU city stays unnameable**: Replit does not document it, so this repository claims EU residency and never Frankfurt, which survives only for the AWS evidence bucket in `eu-central-1` where the region is verifiable.

Four alternatives were rejected. Waiting for the market decision before publishing was rejected because Phase 2 cannot proceed without a published environment — the production database does not exist until the first publish — and the decision has no due date, so waiting is indefinite in exchange for nothing this decision does not already buy. Publishing to North America because the parent is Canadian was rejected twice over: it is US hosting, not Canadian, and it is the direction that cannot be walked back. Publishing to the EU because the repository always said so was rejected as the version of this decision worth regretting — a premise repeated is not a premise examined, and the point of writing this down is that the next reader finds a reason rather than an inheritance. Treating the geography as revisitable later was rejected because it is not: the platform fixes it permanently at first publish, and the only correction is a new project, which is exactly why the gate is placed before cutover rather than after it.
